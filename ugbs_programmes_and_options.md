# UGBS Undergraduate Programmes and Options (BSc Administration)

This document explains which undergraduate programmes and options the University of Ghana Business School (UGBS) offers, which department runs each one, what each option involves, and how a Level 200 student can choose an option for Level 300.

## Which undergraduate programmes does UGBS offer?

UGBS offers the four-year **BSc Administration** degree with nine options (also called majors), and a two-year **Diploma in Accounting**. The options for the 2026/2027 academic year are:

| Option (major) | Award | Department that runs it |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting | BSc Administration (Accounting) | Department of Accounting |
| Banking and Finance | BSc Administration (Banking and Finance) | Department of Finance |
| Insurance | BSc Administration (Insurance) | Department of Finance |
| Marketing | BSc Administration (Marketing) | Department of Marketing and Entrepreneurship |
| E-Commerce and Customer Management | BSc Administration (E-Commerce and Customer Management) | Department of Marketing and Entrepreneurship |
| Human Resource Management | BSc Administration (Human Resource Management) | Department of Organisation and Human Resource Management |
| Health Services Management | BSc Administration (Health Services Management) | Health Services Management (part of the Department of Public Administration and Health Services Management in older documents) |
| Public Administration | BSc Administration (Public Administration) | Public Administration (part of the Department of Public Administration and Health Services Management in older documents) |
| Analytics (Business Analytics) | BSc Administration (Analytics) | Department of Operations and Management Information Systems (OMIS) |

UGBS has six academic departments: Accounting; Finance; Marketing and Entrepreneurship; Organisation and Human Resource Management (OHRM); Operations and Management Information Systems (OMIS); and Public Administration and Health Services Management (PAHSM) -- the last of these is a single combined department covering both areas, confirmed via UGBS's own official pages and a UGBS departmental-leadership announcement. Health Services Management and Public Administration are options/majors within PAHSM, not separate departments.

## When do I choose my option?

The BSc Administration degree has a common foundation in Levels 100 and 200, and the specialised courses of each option begin at Level 300. This is how the 2017 undergraduate handbook sets out the programme structure, and it matches the way UGBS held its Level 300 orientation for the Analytics option. A Level 200 student therefore chooses an option for Level 300. Course lists and rules can change, so students should confirm the current arrangement, and any requirements for entering an option, with their department and the Academic Office.

## What do Levels 100 and 200 cover?

Based on the 2017 handbook (the current course list may differ):

- **Level 100:** university required courses such as Academic Writing and Critical Thinking; Introduction to Business Administration; Introduction to Public Administration; Introduction to Computing in Business; Principles of Management; Psychology for Everyday Living; and an introductory foreign language (French or Chinese).
- **Level 200:** Academic Writing II; Microeconomics and Business; Macroeconomics and Business; Business Mathematics; Commercial Law I and II; Fundamentals of Accounting Methods; Introduction to Financial Accounting; Social Responsibility and Ethics; Introduction to African Studies.

Courses shared by the options in the 2017 handbook at Levels 300 and 400 include Quantitative Methods (UGBS 301), Research Methods (UGBS 302), Computer Applications in Management (UGBS 303), Company Law (UGBS 401) and Business Policy (UGBS 402).

## What does each option involve?

### Accounting
Knowledge and skills in accounting and financial reporting: financial reporting, auditing, taxation, public sector and management accounting, and accounting information systems. It suits students who like accuracy, rules and detail, and it is a foundation for professional accounting qualifications. UGBS describes Accounting as the first programme of choice for students since the school began. The department also runs the two-year Diploma in Accounting.
Level 300 courses in the 2017 handbook include Introduction to Financial Reporting, Financial Reporting, Auditing, Introduction to Business Finance, Business Finance and Managerial Economics, with an elective such as Principles of Marketing or Fundamentals of Entrepreneurship.

### Banking and Finance
Banking, financial markets, financial management, investment and corporate finance. It suits students who enjoy numbers, markets and risk, and it prepares them for banks, financial services, investment and corporate finance roles. The Department of Finance describes its programmes as the most sought after in UGBS, with demand higher than the places available.

### Insurance
Insurance principles, risk management, insurance operations and related financial services. It is run by the Department of Finance and suits students interested in risk and financial protection.

### Marketing
Marketing principles, consumer behaviour, marketing strategy, communication, sales and retail. It suits students who are creative and persuasive and like understanding customers. The Department of Marketing and Entrepreneurship describes itself as the oldest marketing department in Ghana.
Level 300 and 400 courses in the 2017 handbook include Principles of Marketing, Introduction to Electronic Business, Consumer Behaviour, Sales Management, Integrated Marketing Communications, Fundamentals of Entrepreneurship, Retail Strategy, Marketing Research, Marketing Management and Fundamentals of Social Marketing.

### E-Commerce and Customer Management
Electronic business, digital commerce, customer management and the use of technology in business. It suits students who like technology, online business and customer experience.
Level 300 and 400 courses in the 2017 handbook include Customer Care Essentials, Customer Relationship Management, Mobile Commerce and Marketing, Introduction to E-Service Management, Innovation and New Product Development, Measuring Customer Satisfaction, Key Account Management and Public Sector Marketing Management.

### Human Resource Management
Managing people in organisations: human resource practices, organisational behaviour, recruitment, development, labour law and industrial relations. It is aimed at entry-level HR roles in private, public and not-for-profit organisations. It suits students who are good with people and like solving people problems.
Level 300 and 400 courses in the 2017 handbook include Human Behaviour in Organisation, Labour Law, Introduction to Human Resource Management, Labour Economics and Manpower Planning.

### Health Services Management
Management and administration of hospitals, clinics and other health organisations, combining business administration with the needs of the health sector. It suits students who are service-minded and interested in health institutions. Courses in the 2017 handbook include Foundations of Health Promotion, Healthcare Financing and Principles of Healthcare Quality Assurance.

### Public Administration
Administration and management of public organisations: public sector management, governance, policy implementation and organisational operations. The department prepares students for leadership and management roles in national and international public organisations. It suits students interested in government, institutions and public service.

### Analytics (Business Analytics)
A business-focused option for using data, analytical methods, technology and quantitative techniques to support decisions. It was introduced in the 2024/2025 academic year and is run by the Department of Operations and Management Information Systems (OMIS). Focus areas include business analytics, data analysis, business intelligence, data-driven decision-making and quantitative problem-solving. It suits students who enjoy numbers, logic and technology. Students have their own association, the Business Analytics Students Association (BASA), and UGBS has said it is partnering with other institutions to provide internships. The programme has a designated course advisor in OMIS. A detailed Analytics course list is not included in this document, so students should ask OMIS for the current list.

## How can I choose the right option?

Think about three questions: what kind of work you enjoy day to day, how comfortable you are with mathematics, statistics and software tools, and where you would like to work after graduating. The assistant has a short guided questionnaire under the "Choose an option" topic. It asks about your interests and strengths and suggests two or three options to consider. It is a starting point, not a decision or a prediction of your results. Before deciding, talk to the course advisor or office of the department that runs the option, and confirm the current course list, entry requirements and deadlines with the Academic Office.

## Q: Which level do I choose my major or option at UGBS?
A: At UGBS, all BSc Administration students choose their major (option) at Level 300. Levels 100 and 200 cover common core business courses taken by all students regardless of option. At Level 300, you declare your option — such as Accounting, Finance, Marketing, Human Resource Management, Analytics, Public Administration, or Health Services Management — and your courses become option-specific from that point.

## Q: Can I change my option after choosing at Level 300?
A: A change of option is treated as a change of programme and requires approval from the Academic Affairs Directorate. Contact your department office or the UGBS Undergraduate Academic Office first to understand whether the switch is feasible given your credit history.
