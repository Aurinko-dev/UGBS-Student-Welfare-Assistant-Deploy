# University of Ghana Business School — Business House Junior Common Room (BHJCR) Constitution

Source: The Constitution of the Business House Junior Common Room, University of Ghana Business School, University of Ghana, Legon. Approved by the 14th General Assembly, made 29th September 2016. [VERIFIED — uploaded by user from official BHJCR document]

Note: The BHJCR is UGBS's student government body (equivalent to a JCR at other halls/schools). It operates under, and is subject to, the SRC Constitution, the University of Ghana Statutes, and the laws of Ghana. This document is UGBS-specific — it is not the university-wide SRC Constitution.

## What is the BHJCR and who is a member?
The Business House Junior Common Room (BHJCR) is the student government of the University of Ghana Business School. Membership comprises all undergraduate students of UGBS, on both Main Campus and Accra City Campus. Its stated aims are to seek the welfare and protect the rights of its members, foster good relationships between members and school authorities, and serve as the mouthpiece of its members.

## Who runs the BHJCR? (Arms and Organs)
Note: "President of the Business School," "UGBS President," and "BHJCR President" all refer to the same office — the President of the Business House Junior Common Room, described below.

There are three arms: the Executive, the Judiciary, and the Legislature.
There are several organs: the Audit Board, the Electoral Commission, the Council of Representatives and Welfare Committee, the Finance Committee, the Public Relations Committee, the Council of Representatives of Students' Associations, the Endowment Board, and the Business Journal Board.

## The Executive (day-to-day leadership)
The Executive comprises: President (Main Campus), Vice President (Main Campus), Vice President (Accra City Campus), Secretary, Financial Controller, Treasurer, Organising Secretary, and Coordinator (Accra City Campus). They are elected annually, hold office for one year, and are not eligible for re-election. The President is the Leader and Chief Executive of the BHJCR and represents it in legal proceedings.

## Q: Who do I go to for a student welfare issue in UGBS?
A: The Council of Representatives and Welfare Committee is specifically charged with matters of welfare (e.g. death of a JCR member) and with discussing problems raised by classes/departments and recommending action to the General Assembly. Broader welfare and rights protection is also a stated object of the BHJCR as a whole, carried out day-to-day by the Executive.

## Q: What is the Council of Representatives and Welfare Committee?
A: It comprises three class representatives each from levels 100, 200, 300, and 400, from both Main Campus and Accra City Campus. Members are elected via the Electoral Commission and vetted by the General Assembly. Its functions include: handling welfare matters, organising classes/departments for JCR activities, discussing class/department problems and making recommendations to the General Assembly, and disseminating information from the General Assembly to classes and departments.

## Q: How do I raise a complaint or grievance within the BHJCR?
A: Complaints can be raised through your class representative on the Council of Representatives and Welfare Committee, who escalates issues to the General Assembly. For matters involving alleged misconduct or breaches of the constitution, the Judiciary has jurisdiction to hear and determine such matters.

## The General Assembly (highest decision-making body)
The General Assembly is the highest decision-making body of the BHJCR, comprising the Executive, Judiciary, Legislature, Audit Board, Electoral Commission, and representatives of the Journal Board and Students' Associations. It meets at least three times per semester and decides issues concerning BHJCR members.

## The Judiciary (disciplinary and constitutional matters)
The Judiciary comprises seven members (five Main Campus, two Accra City Campus), headed by a Chief Justice, with other members called Justices. It handles: enforcement/interpretation of the constitution, disputes over whether a person or authority exceeded their powers, election-related matters, and impeachment proceedings against Executive Officers. Its decisions on constitutional interpretation are final and conclusive, though appealable to the SRC Judicial Committee. Any person appearing before the Judiciary has the right to counsel of their choice.

## Q: What happens if a BHJCR officer misuses funds or misbehaves?
A: The Audit Board audits BHJCR accounts annually and can demand explanations from any person, including the Executive. If funds are believed to be misappropriated, an investigative committee is convened and may recommend disciplinary measures to the Judiciary or General Assembly. Grounds for removing an Executive Officer include: violating the oath of office or the constitution, conduct that brings the BHJCR into disrepute, incapacity, embezzlement/misappropriation of funds, or inefficiency/negligence in duties. Removal requires a written notice signed by at least one-third of the General Assembly, a Judiciary finding of a prima facie case, and a two-thirds majority vote of the General Assembly by secret ballot (impeachment process, Article 56).

## Elections
BHJCR Executive elections are held in the first semester of each academic year, run by the Electoral Commission (a Chairperson/"Electoral Commissioner" plus three Deputy Commissioners). Winners are decided by simple plurality (first-past-the-post) except where unopposed. A member may challenge the validity of an election in the BHJCR court (Judiciary) within seven days of the result being declared. Term of office is one year.

## Q: Is there financial support for students in exceptional hardship, via the BHJCR?
A: The BHJCR's own constitution does not establish a hardship fund; the Financial Controller and Treasurer manage BHJCR dues and an imprest for BHJCR operations, not individual student welfare grants. For financial hardship support, the correct channel is the University's Financial Aid Office (SFAO) and the Office of the Dean of UGBS (see ugbs_academic_policies.md), not the BHJCR directly — though a student's class representative or the Welfare Committee can help raise and escalate such concerns.

## Amendments and governing hierarchy
The BHJCR constitution is subject to (i.e. cannot override) the SRC Constitution of the University of Ghana, the Statutes of the University, and the laws of Ghana. Amendments require support from at least 20 BHJCR members, Judiciary review, and a two-thirds majority vote of the Legislature.
