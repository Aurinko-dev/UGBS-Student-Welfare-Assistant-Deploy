# University of Ghana — Sexual Harassment & Misconduct: Reporting and Support

Source: University of Ghana Sexual Harassment and Misconduct Policy (Special Reporter No. 873, 5 May 2017), published by CEGENSA — the Centre for Gender Studies and Advocacy (cegensa.ug.edu.gh). Full policy PDF verified directly. [VERIFIED]

Important: this file covers reporting channels, rights, and support — not a detailed catalogue of harassment examples. If a student's message suggests they may be describing or experiencing harassment, prioritize pointing them to CEGENSA and the reporting process below over anything else.

## What counts as sexual harassment or misconduct, briefly
The policy covers unwelcome conduct of a sexual nature — including unwanted advances, requests for sexual favours, and verbal, written, electronic, or physical conduct — when it is made a condition of academic standing or employment, used as a basis for academic/employment decisions, or creates an intimidating or hostile environment. It also covers other sexual misconduct: sexual abuse, sexual assault, sexual intimidation (including stalking), and exploitative behaviour, including relationships where a power imbalance (e.g. lecturer–student) makes "consent" questionable.

## Q: Who handles sexual harassment reports at UG?
A: The Anti-Sexual Harassment Committee, supported by CEGENSA (Centre for Gender Studies and Advocacy). CEGENSA also runs a dedicated sexual harassment crisis and counselling unit for students and staff who have experienced harassment or assault. Contact CEGENSA directly: https://cegensa.ug.edu.gh

## Q: How do I report an incident?
A: Two routes:
1. **Informal** — you can address the person directly (telling them the behaviour is unwelcome and must stop), ask a trusted person to intervene on your behalf, or request mediation through the Committee. Informal resolution is optional, not required, and choosing not to use it does not weaken a later formal complaint.
2. **Formal** — bring your complaint to the Anti-Sexual Harassment Committee. You present your grievance (orally or in writing — the Committee assists if you're unable to write), the Respondent is notified and given a chance to respond in writing, and an Adjudication Committee hears both sides. The process is meant to complete within 60 working days.

For severe cases (e.g. rape, sexual assault, assault with a weapon), the policy specifically advises reporting to the Police in addition to filing with the Committee, rather than relying on informal resolution.

## Q: What are my rights if I report something?
A: You will not be reprimanded, retaliated against, or discriminated against for reporting in good faith. Proceedings are kept confidential. You have the right to representation by counsel (though counsel cannot speak on your behalf during proceedings). You may withdraw a filed complaint at any time, in writing. The Committee actively monitors for retaliation during an investigation, and retaliation is itself treated as a new report of misconduct.

## Q: What if I'm not sure it counted as harassment, or don't want to file a formal complaint?
A: You can still contact CEGENSA or the Careers and Counselling Directorate for confidential support and to talk through your options — you are not required to file a formal complaint to get support.

## Q: What happens to someone found to have violated the policy?
A: Sanctions are decided case-by-case and can include a formal apology, leave without pay, suspension, denial of promotion, demotion, transfer, or dismissal — dismissal in serious or repeat cases. These sanctions don't prevent separate criminal prosecution where the conduct is also a crime under Ghanaian law. Either party can appeal an outcome to the University of Ghana Appeals Board.

## Recommended office
CEGENSA (Centre for Gender Studies and Advocacy) — https://cegensa.ug.edu.gh — is the primary point of contact. The Careers and Counselling Directorate is also available for confidential emotional support alongside, or instead of, filing a formal complaint.
