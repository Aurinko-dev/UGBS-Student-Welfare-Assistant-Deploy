# University of Ghana — Student Discipline & Complaint Governance

Source: Statutes of the University of Ghana, August 2024 (ug.edu.gh/pad). [VERIFIED]

## Q: Who is ultimately responsible for discipline at the University of Ghana?
A: The Vice-Chancellor is the Chief Disciplinary Officer of the University, ultimately responsible for setting disciplinary standards, initiating and overseeing disciplinary proceedings, and preserving disciplinary integrity.

## Q: What counts as misconduct at UG?
A: Misconduct is unacceptable or improper behaviour that amounts to a failure to perform an assigned duty, a breach of University laws, or conduct that brings the University into disrepute. This includes:
- Plagiarism and examination malpractice
- Cheating or impersonation in exams
- Awarding or procuring marks for a course not taken
- Sexual or other harassment
- Scandalous, immoral, or disgraceful conduct
- Criminal conviction
- Electoral fraud or malpractice
- Defamation of University officers
Gross Misconduct is misconduct serious enough to amount to a fundamental breach of trust and confidence.

## Q: What is the difference between academic and non-academic misconduct?
A: Academic misconduct (Statute 73) covers violations related to examinations and academic work — plagiarism, cheating, impersonation, falsifying results, and procuring marks fraudulently. Non-academic misconduct (Statute 74) covers behaviour outside academic work — harassment, defamation, violence, theft, damage to property, and conduct bringing the University into disrepute.

## Q: What happens when a misconduct complaint is filed against a student?
A: The process follows this escalation chain:
1. Complaint is lodged with the relevant authority
2. The Disciplinary Board for Junior Members (students) holds a hearing
3. The Board investigates and establishes whether misconduct occurred
4. Sanctions are recommended to the Vice-Chancellor
5. A formal written notice is issued by the Registrar
Where the University already has evidence, a formal investigation may not be required before charging the student directly.

## Q: What sanctions can be imposed on a student found guilty of misconduct?
A: Sanctions are governed by Statute 76 and range from a written warning to suspension or expulsion depending on severity. For example, in January 2023, four Commonwealth Hall JCR executives were each suspended for three academic years for publishing defamatory statements against University officers.

## Q: What happens if I get caught cheating in an exam?
A: Examination malpractice is treated as academic misconduct under Statute 73. You will be reported to the Disciplinary Board for Junior Members. Sanctions can include a zero for the paper, cancellation of all results for that sitting, suspension, or expulsion depending on severity. Track your case status via the STS portal or email bcsecretariat@ug.edu.gh.

## Q: Can a student appeal a disciplinary decision?
A: Yes. Statute 78 establishes the University of Ghana Appeals Board for university-level appeals. Statute 79 requires exhaustion of internal remedies first — you must go through the University's own appeal process before seeking external redress.

## Q: Is there an amnesty option for students with disciplinary issues?
A: Yes. Statute 77 provides for amnesty. Amnesty windows are advertised on the University website (ug.edu.gh) and college notice boards. To apply, follow the instructions in the advert and contact the Academic Affairs Directorate at aad@ug.edu.gh or Room D10, Left Wing of the Registry. The BHJCR and SRC do not process university amnesty applications.

## Q: Where do I report a misconduct complaint?
A: Depending on the nature:
- Academic misconduct (cheating, plagiarism): your Head of Department or AAD at aad@ug.edu.gh
- Examination malpractice: Teaching and Examinations Unit at teu-academic@ug.edu.gh
- Sexual harassment or GBV: CEGENSA (near LT3) or the Anti-Sexual Harassment Committee
- General student misconduct: Office of the Dean of Student Affairs (near Balme Library, same premises as the University Post Office)

## Q: Where do I check the status of my examination malpractice case?
A: Check for a push notification on the STS portal, or email bcsecretariat@ug.edu.gh with your student ID and case details.

## Q: Where does discipline sit in the UG Statutes?
A: The relevant Statutes are:
- Statute 66: Office of Student Affairs
- Statute 71: Student Governance
- Statute 73: Academic Misconduct
- Statute 74: Non-Academic Misconduct
- Statute 75: Disciplinary Bodies and Rules of Procedure
- Statute 76: Sanctions
- Statute 77: Amnesty
- Statute 78: The University of Ghana Appeals Board
- Statute 79: Exhaustion of Internal Remedies
