# University of Ghana — Statutes: Governance & Student-Relevant Provisions

Source: "Amendments to the Statutes of the University of Ghana," University of Ghana, published September 2016 (amending the base Statutes of 1 May 2011; passed by Council 26 June 2014). Official document, ug.edu.gh. [VERIFIED]

Important note: this document amends the 2011 Statutes — it is not the complete original Statutes text. It is used here for the governance facts most relevant to a student-facing welfare assistant: who holds authority over discipline, academic affairs, and student affairs at the university level (above UGBS/BHJCR level).

## University academic structure (for context)
Academic Divisions of the University are Colleges, Schools, Institutes, Centres, and Departments. UGBS ("Business School") sits as a School within the College of Humanities, alongside Schools of Arts, Law, Languages, Performing Arts, and Social Sciences.
- A College is headed by a Provost (3-year term, renewable once).
- A School (like UGBS) is headed by a Dean, who must be of professorial status, appointed by Council for a 3-year term (renewable once).
- A Department is headed by a Head of Department, appointed for up to 3 years.

## Q: Who is the Dean of Student Affairs and what changed about their role?
A: The Statutes establish a Dean of Student Affairs role (Statute 38). The 2016 amendments deleted Statute 38(2)(a) and (b) — i.e., specific sub-clauses were removed — but the amendment document itself does not restate what the Dean of Student Affairs' remaining duties are in full; for the complete current description of the office, the base 2011 Statutes (not just this amendment) or the university's Dean of Student Affairs webpage would need to be consulted directly.

## Q: Who handles student discipline at the university level?
A: Discipline is governed by Statute 43 ("Rules and Procedures Relating to Discipline"). Key points from the 2016 amendments:
- A disciplinary board or committee investigates allegations of misconduct referred to it by the **Chief Disciplinary Officer** or the Registrar, and makes decisions including sanctions.
- Where the University already possesses relevant evidence, a formal investigation is not required before charging the accused person directly before the disciplinary committee.
- Charges must be in writing, addressed to the accused, signed by or under the authority of the Chief Disciplinary Officer, filed with the Registrar, who then notifies the Chairman and Secretary of the disciplinary body.
- The University's Legal Counsel is responsible for prosecuting persons accused of breaching any enactment.
- Disciplinary Boards/Committees make recommendations for sanctions to the Vice-Chancellor.

## The Registrar and Director of Academic Affairs
- The **Registrar** has administrative oversight of the "Registrary" (the university's central administrative machinery), including advising on student registration/admission materials, academic calendars, class schedules, timetables, grade sheets, and distribution of semester reports.
- The **Director of Academic Affairs** operates under the Pro-Vice-Chancellor (Academic and Student Affairs) and is responsible for implementing the university's academic policy, including preparation/publication of academic materials such as the Handbook of Regulations for students (in consultation with Legal Counsel and the Director of Public Affairs).

## Office of Legal Counsel (established by these amendments)
Responsible for: legal advice on all university matters; drafting/reviewing/interpreting university agreements; representing the university in litigation; supervising outside legal services; facilitating the work of the university's Appeals Board; and reviewing university activities for legal compliance.

## Q: Where does academic appeal or grievance ultimately go, at the university level?
A: The Statutes reference an Appeals Board of the university (facilitated by the Office of Legal Counsel) as the higher body for university-level appeals, separate from College/School-level Academic Boards and Management Committees, which handle programme-level academic matters (course regulations, examinations, degree awards) subject to review by the University Academic Board.

## Governance hierarchy (for context on where student bodies fit)
University Council → Vice-Chancellor → Provosts (Colleges) → Deans (Schools, e.g. UGBS) → Heads of Department. Student representation is built into this structure: e.g., College Advisory Boards and Management Committees of Schools/Institutes each include one undergraduate student representative appointed by the SRC, and one postgraduate representative appointed by the Graduate Students Association.
