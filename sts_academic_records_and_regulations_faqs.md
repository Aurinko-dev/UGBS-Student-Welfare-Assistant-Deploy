# Registration, Grading, Exams, Results and Deferment (STS FAQs)

Source: sts.ug.edu.gh FAQ page, summarised from screenshots. [Verify against sts.ug.edu.gh and Academic Affairs before treating as final.] Some answers are written for College of Humanities students, including distance learners.

## Registration and Courses

### Q: What do I do if I want to add or drop a course after add/drop or registration is over?
A: Go to your College Academic Office.

### Q: The handbook says a minimum of 15 credits per semester, but I am in Level 100 and could only register my three required courses. What do I do?
A: Besides your three required courses, register one University Required Course (UGRC) and one Humanities and Education Required Course (HERC) each semester.

### Q: How many University Required Courses (UGRCs) must I take, and which ones?
A: 4 UGRCs: UGRC110, UGRC150, UGRC210 and UGRC220-239 (all students). (Regulations p. 44.)

### Q: How many College Required Courses (HERCs) must I take in the College of Humanities?
A: 2 HERCs. Students who choose HERCs from the Department of Modern Languages take 2 courses per semester.

### Q: What should I do if I register for a course and can't write the exam?
A: You may defer the course by writing to the Academic Affairs Officer through the Centre Head, stating why you could not write.

### Q: What do I do when my courses clash on the exam timetable?
A: Report it to the Academic Affairs Directorate (Room D7).

### Q: I wrote a paper but the lecturer says they can't trace my ID number to enter my mark.
A: Contact your Academic Officer for assistance or further investigation.

### Q: How do I know if my registration/grade issue is resolved?
A: Check your academic records on MIS Web online. You may also email the Academic Affairs Office.

## Grading and Academic Records

### Q: What is the grading scale for Level 100 courses?
A: Level 100 courses are handled by the College of Basic and Applied Sciences (CBAS) on the Legon campus.

| Grade | Marks |
|---|---|
| A | 80-100 |
| B+ | 75-79 |
| B | 70-74 |
| C+ | 65-69 |
| C | 60-64 |
| D+ | 55-59 |
| D | 50-59 (as shown; the Level 200-400 table gives 50-54) |
| E | 45-49 |
| F | 0-44 |

### Q: What is the grading scale and grade point for Level 200-400 courses?
A: Grading scale and grade points for Level 200-400:

| Letter | Marks | Grade point |
|---|---|---|
| A | 80-100 | 4.0 |
| B+ | 75-79 | 3.5 |
| B | 70-74 | 3.0 |
| C+ | 65-69 | 2.5 |
| C | 60-64 | 2.0 |
| D+ | 55-59 | 1.5 |
| D | 50-54 | 1.0 |
| E | 45-49 | 0.5 |
| F | 0-44 | 0 |

Grades A-C are pass grades. D, F, X and Z are failure grades. (Regulations p. 46.)

### Q: What is a Grade Point (GP)?
A: The grade points earned for a course are the number of credits for the course multiplied by the grade point equivalent of the grade obtained.

### Q: How do I calculate my GPAs?
A: Total GPTs / Total credits.

### Q: What is a Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA)?
A: Total grade points obtained up to a given time, divided by the total credits of all courses registered up to that time.

### Q: What is a Final Grade Point Average (FGPA)?
A: The CGPA for all courses under consideration, calculated up to the end of a student's academic programme.

### Q: What are the degree classifications at the University of Ghana (by FGPA)?
A: Degree classes are awarded by FGPA:

| FGPA | Class |
|---|---|
| 3.60-4.00 | First Class Honours |
| 3.00-3.59 | Second Class Honours (Upper Division) |
| 2.00-2.99 | Second Class Honours (Lower Division) |
| 1.50-1.99 | Third Class Honours |
| 1.00-1.49 | Pass |
| 0.00-0.99 | Fail |

(Regulations p. 50.)

### Q: Why does my last CGPA show a first/second class that doesn't match my FGPA?
A: The last CGPA on your academic record is not the same as the FGPA. The FGPA is calculated at the end of Level 400 and always shows on your transcript. Class is awarded at the end of Level 400 using the weighting 1:1:2:2.

### Q: I have been shortchanged. My final-semester CGPA is 3.20 but I am listed as Second Lower.
A: You have not been shortchanged. Class is determined by the FGPA, not the CGPA. The CGPA uses equal weighting, while the FGPA uses a ratio of 1:1:2:2. (Regulations p. 50.)

### Q: What is the difference between an academic record and an official transcript?
A: The academic record shows grades, CCT/CCP, GPAs and CGPAs for every semester and level. Students access it through the MIS and it can be called the student's copy of the transcript. The official transcript is issued by the University on request and shows all the relevant information.

### Q: What should I do if there are errors in the calculation of my CCT and CCP?
A: Report to the Academic Officers of your college.

### Q: What should I do if the credit hour and GPA are not reflecting on my academic record?
A: Report to the Academic Officers of your college.

## Exams, Results and Complaints

### Q: What do I do when other colleagues have received their results but I haven't received mine?
A: Visit the department concerned and fill out a complaint form, available in the general office.

### Q: My results are not showing on my academic record. What should I do?
A: Report to your Departmental Examinations Officer and fill out a complaint form.

### Q: If my grade is not showing on my academic record, what should I do?
A: Report to the department where you took the course. You can contact the Head of Department or the Departmental Examinations Officer.

### Q: If I am not satisfied with my result, where should I lodge a complaint?
A: Apply to the Director, Academic Affairs Directorate, not later than 21 days after the release of results, for a review/remarking. A review fee applies.

### Q: What is the procedure for remarking?
A: Apply to the Director, Academic Affairs Directorate, not later than 21 days after the release of results. A review fee applies.

### Q: What are the opportunities for a student to redeem a failure grade?
A: Students who get failure grades can re-write the papers during the period set aside for supplementary examinations.

### Q: Is there a limit to the number of failures one can have and still qualify for supplementary examination?
A: A student who fails not more than five courses at the end of the academic year can re-write them as a supplementary examination. A student who fails more than five is asked to repeat.

### Q: What happens if I get a failure grade in a supplementary examination?
A: You would be asked to repeat the academic year.

### Q: What are the requirements for progression to the next level?
A: Except for students in the School of Nursing and Midwifery, a student can proceed to the next stage only if they have passed all modules of the preceding level.

### Q: I wrote a course and got a D but I don't like it. Can I re-sit it?
A: No. Under the new grading system the regulations only allow re-sits for courses graded E and F. You cannot re-sit a course where you scored better than these two grades.

## Deferment, Programme Changes and Learning Centres

### Q: How do I defer my course of study or programme?
A: Give written notice to the Dean of your School and wait for approval before you defer.

Note: this describes the written-notice route. UGBS continuing students may be directed to the Deferment Form on the STS Portal (see ugbs_academic_policies.md). If the two differ, follow the current process shown on the STS Portal or confirm with the Academic Affairs Directorate.

### Q: How do I resume academic work after deferment?
A: Students in Accra present the deferment approval letter to the Academic Affairs Directorate to get a resumption letter. Students outside Accra send a copy of the deferment approval letter through their Centre Heads to the College Academic Affairs Office, which arranges to send them the resumption letter. Students must also register online.

### Q: Can I change my programme in the course of my study?
A: Not on your own. You may do it through the Dean of your School and the Dean of the School where the intended programme is offered.

### Q: I will not be in my Learning Centre during the examinations. Can I write at any Learning Centre near me?
A: Yes, but you must write a letter requesting permission through your current Learning Centre at least two weeks before the start of examinations.

### Q: How do I change my Learning Centre?
A: Write a letter to the Academic Office (COE) through your current Learning Centre, stating your reasons and attaching relevant documents.

## Q: How do I calculate my GPA at the University of Ghana?
A: UG uses a 4.0 grade point scale. To calculate your semester GPA:

**Step 1 — Know the grading scale:**
| Grade | Mark (%) | Grade Point |
|---|---|---|
| A | 80-100 | 4.0 |
| B+ | 75-79 | 3.5 |
| B | 70-74 | 3.0 |
| C+ | 65-69 | 2.5 |
| C | 60-64 | 2.0 |
| D+ | 55-59 | 1.5 |
| D | 50-54 | 1.0 |
| E | 45-49 | 0.5 |
| F | 0-44 | 0.0 |

**Step 2 — Calculate Grade Points (GPT) per course:**
Multiply each course's credit hours by its grade point value.
Example: B+ (3.5) in a 3-credit course = 3.5 x 3 = 10.5 GPT

**Step 3 — Sum all GPTs and divide by total credit hours:**
Semester GPA = Total GPT / Total Credit Hours

**CGPA vs FGPA:**
- CGPA (Cumulative GPA): Overall average across all semesters — sum of all GPTs divided by total credit hours taken so far
- FGPA (Final GPA): Determines your degree class. UG weights levels differently — Level 100 (weight 1), Level 200 (weight 1), Level 300 (weight 2), Level 400 (weight 2)

For a visual walkthrough, watch this tutorial: https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=university+of+ghana+GPA+calculation

For your exact GPA, log into the STS portal at https://sts.ug.edu.gh — your transcript shows your current CGPA.

## Q: What happens if I fail a course?
A: If you fail a course at UGBS, you may be eligible to take a resit examination. First, check your result to confirm the grade you received. If the course is eligible for a resit, register for the resit during the designated registration period and take the examination at the next available opportunity. If you pass the resit, the course requirement can be cleared according to the University's grading rules. If you fail a compulsory course, you will need to pass it before completing your graduation requirements.

To register for the resit: log into the student portal, go to the examination/resit registration section, select the failed course, complete the registration and make any required payment before the deadline. After registering, check the resit examination timetable for the date and venue.

Note: this is separate from missing an exam entirely, which results in a grade of 'X' rather than a failing grade -- see the missed-exam/medical-report process elsewhere in this document for that situation.

## Q: How can I access research papers, academic journals, and research books for my studies at UG?
A: You can access these through the University of Ghana Library System (Balme Library):

**Online databases (from on or off campus):**
- The University subscribes to major academic databases including ScienceDirect, JSTOR, EBSCOhost, Scopus, and Sage Research Methods, giving full-text access to peer-reviewed journals and e-books.
- Off-campus access: log in through the official UG EZProxy Off-Campus Service using your institutional (student) computing credentials to verify your student status.
- UGSpace is the University's open-access institutional repository for past theses, dissertations, and local research publications.

**Physical library spaces at UGBS:**
- UGBS Undergraduate Library (K. E. Adjei Library) -- located at the main UGBS undergraduate building, holds printed textbooks and commerce/government publications.
- UGBS Graduate Library (Tullow Library) -- located at the UGBS Graduate Building, for graduate-level research.
- Knowledge Commons (KC) -- a computer lab on the ground floor of the East Wing of the main Balme Library, useful for digital research and searching databases.

**Other resources:**
- Past examination papers are available through the library's Online Past Questions Service at the library help desks.
- The library offers support and training for reference management tools such as Mendeley and EndNote.

If you have forgotten your login password for off-campus access, this is handled by University IT Support (ITS). Note: Ghanaian and international students may be issued credentials through slightly different processes, so before recommending where to reset a password or resolve an access issue, ask whether the student is Ghanaian or an international student, and confirm the current process with the Balme Library help desk or ITS if the two differ.

Recommended office: Balme Library / UGBS Library staff for access issues; University IT Support (ITS) for password resets -- confirm nationality (Ghanaian vs. international student) first, as escalation contacts may differ.
