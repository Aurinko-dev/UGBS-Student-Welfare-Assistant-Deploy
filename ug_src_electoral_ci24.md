# University of Ghana SRC — Electoral Constitutional Instrument (C.I. 24)

Source: "Constitutional Instrument (C.I. 24)," University of Ghana Students' Representative Council (SRC) Electoral Commission, effective for the 2024 SRC elections, published univers.ug.edu.gh (UG's campus radio station site), dated 24th June 2024. [VERIFIED]

Important note: this document governs SRC *elections* specifically. It is not the complete SRC Constitution (membership rules, officer duties beyond elections, General Assembly powers) — that full text was not found as a public document; if you have a copy, it should be added separately.

## What is C.I. 24?
It is the Electoral Commission's rulebook for conducting SRC elections electronically (an "online-offsite" voting system run by University of Ghana Computing Systems, UGCS), enacted under Article 38 of the UG SRC Constitution (2012). It covers all elections within SRC territory, including JCR, Department, and Satellite campus elections.

## Q: Who can vote in SRC elections and how?
A: All students at the University of Ghana are eligible voters ("universal suffrage" — regardless of gender, race, ethnicity, religion). Voting is electronic and "online-offsite," meaning students vote remotely (from halls, homes, etc.) via software run by UGCS, not at a physical polling booth. Voting runs from 8:00am to 4:00pm on election day (barring unforeseen circumstances), held on the same day across the University.

## Q: How does someone become a candidate, and can they be disqualified?
A: Nomination follows Article 29 of the SRC Constitution. A Vetting Committee (constituted per Article 41 of the SRC Constitution, open to the general student population) can disqualify a candidate only if they don't meet the SRC Constitution's eligibility requirements, or if they voluntarily withdraw. For Presidential/Vice-Presidential tickets, if either running mate fails eligibility, the whole ticket is disqualified.

## Security and integrity measures
Encryption of data, voter data protection, biometric authentication (to prevent double voting), audit trails, and regular penetration testing. Accredited security personnel only (Ghana Police, internal security, student cadets) are permitted — unaccredited security is barred from the process.

## Q: What if I have a complaint about how an SRC election was conducted?
A: During voting, complaints go to the Chairperson of the Electoral Commission. Polling agents with process issues must file a complaint form attached to the declaration sheet, addressed in the "strong room." After investigating complaints, the Commission can annul results and call re-elections. Anyone dissatisfied with the arbitration process retains the right to seek further redress (this does not halt the Commission's declaration of results). More broadly, any person dissatisfied with the conduct of an election may challenge it in accordance with the SRC Constitution.

## Results
The Electoral Commission publishes provisional results within 24 hours of polls closing. The Chairperson of the Electoral Commission serves as the Chief Returning Officer.

## Key university stakeholders named in the process
The Commission is required to actively engage stakeholders including the Office of the Dean of Student Affairs, UGCS, JCR commissioners, departments, media, civil society, and security — including a face-to-face candidate debate and a radio face-off (in collaboration with Radio Univers).
